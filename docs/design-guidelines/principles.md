# Principles

## One product, native behavior

- **P1 · Follow Apple for continuity and system boundaries.** Respect safe areas, text scaling, focus and assistive settings. Follow the host's Back, keyboard, picker, permission and sharing conventions. A pushed detail returns to its trigger or logical successor and restores scope and scroll position. Do not simulate Apple's glass or system chrome on content.
- **P2 · Follow Material for hierarchy and the state system.** Use semantic surface, text, selection, focus and status roles. A button, passive chip and destructive control have visibly different treatments. A selected or disabled state is evident with the accent removed. Do not copy Android navigation chrome or let platform-derived color override the app's chosen roles.
- **P3 · Blend for identity.** Use one kit vocabulary on all hosts. Let the task and business choose density, type roles, media and one high-attention moment. Make a screen recognizable without its accent by showing a real domain detail (for example, an issue's asset tag, a slot's remaining seats, or a walk's pickup window). Do not repeat identical rounded tiles, center everything, or use emoji and gradients as substitutes for information.

## Compose and read

- **P4 · Lay out around the task.** In the initial 390-wide view, put identity and current state before details. Keep the primary action reachable with safe area and keyboard; pinned actions must not cover the last row. Use kit rails and gutters, align repeated edges and numeric columns, and make group gaps larger than intra-group gaps. Only center a cohesive standalone moment, not a working list or form. Use one focal point per screen, with one primary action for the current decision.
- **P5 · Use typographic roles.** Use at most three type sizes in an ordinary component (per repeated item in a complex collection), counting action labels. Put tabular figures on a common end edge; keep the unit and qualifier readable. Allow important labels and instructions to wrap. Reflow summaries, rows and actions at large text rather than truncating, shrinking type or clipping. Do not squeeze long numbers or dates into a fixed slot.
- **P6 · Give surfaces and shape a job.** Use a border to separate, a fill to group, a radius to express a surface/control family, and elevation for actual layering. Check boundaries on both light and dark backgrounds. Keep the most emphatic treatment for the primary moment; use a plain rail where cards add no grouping. Use token roles only. Keep semantic good, warning and critical treatments distinct from the accent, with a label or glyph as well as color.
- **P7 · Make imagery useful.** Reserve the media slot and crop before load; ensure fallback preserves identity, time and state. Don't crop out the subject or set live text over uncontrolled contrast. Provide meaningful accessible descriptions for information-bearing media; hide decorative art from assistive technology. Use named catalog glyphs with consistent optical alignment. Pair unfamiliar glyphs with text and give icon-only controls meaningful accessible names; the hit area is larger than the glyph.

## Speak and act honestly

- **P8 · Name a real action and outcome.** Use persistent labels, explicit units and verb-first actions (“Send invite”). Keep record names and status terms stable across sender, receiver, receipt and reader. Errors say what happened and the safe next step without losing the draft. Distinguish first-use empty, filtered empty, caught up, restricted and offline; only offer an action that can run. Say “Request sent” only for a sent request; “Booked” only after confirmation. Inert previews must not look like live controls or promise a real outcome.
- **P9 · Make interaction equivalent.** Use targets of at least 44 pt on iOS, 48 dp on Android and 44 CSS px on web as kit design baselines; compact does not reduce targets. Check 4.5:1 normal text, 3:1 large text and 3:1 for necessary control/state boundaries against adjacent colors, including focus. Keep reading and focus order meaningful, name role and state, announce meaningful change once, and restore focus after dismissing an overlay. Provide a visible alternative to drag, hold or swipe when the action does not inherently require proof of a hold. Never rely on color, motion, sound or haptics alone.
- **P10 · Show an honest first frame.** Open to realistic working content when available; reserve structural space while loading. First usable frame and reduced-motion result communicate the same object, state and controls. The [motion contract](motion.md) explains what changed without making a person wait to act.

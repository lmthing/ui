# Navigation bars, tab bars, search, and actions

## Bars and navigation

| Family                    | Anatomy and appropriate use                                                                                                            | 390-wide behavior                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Status bar                | System time, radios, battery, and other indicators. Maintain a quiet backdrop.                                                         | Don't duplicate it. Hide it temporarily only when the task benefits (such as full-screen video); restore it on exit.                                                                                                                                                    |
| Navigation bar            | Current destination title, Back for hierarchy, Close for dismissal, and a small set of current-view actions.                           | Keep Back at leading; keep one prominent Done or Submit at trailing if needed. Use a concise destination title, not the app name. Let large title become inline on scroll through the native pattern.                                                                   |
| Toolbar                   | Frequently used actions on current content. Group related controls; a More menu holds secondary commands.                              | Try Back, title, and one or two available actions first; reserve room for target hit areas and long labels. Move secondary actions to overflow before shrinking the title or targets. Use top or bottom placement according to the task; don't make both bars compete.  |
| Tab bar                   | Persistent top-level destinations, each with a short label, recognizable icon, and a selected state. Not a toolbar or a create button. | Prototype three to five destinations as a fit check, not a general Apple requirement. Keep positions stable, avoid More when possible, and explain unavailable or empty content inside the destination. Badges signal something worth checking, not routine decoration. |
| Stack or split navigation | A stack pushes detail and pops back to its parent; a split view can show the parent selection and detail together.                     | Prefer the stack on compact iPhone. On wider layouts maintain a visible selection in the list or sidebar when showing detail. Keep independent tab histories and restore list position as a product default.                                                            |

On iOS 26, navigation items can share inset glass by function, and a bottom tab bar may have a search destination or a persistent accessory like a mini-player. Minimize the bar on scroll only when the navigation remains recoverable and the accessory arrangement calls for it. Navigating by tab shouldn't discard a draft or reset the other tabs. A selection indicator should be recognizable without relying on the material or tint.

## Search placement

Use a search field with a scope cue, a clear control, and a result region. For search-heavy apps, use a distinct destination. A standard search tab can open a discovery landing page with suggestions; a separate search-tab appearance can enter the field and keyboard immediately. Toolbar search belongs at the bottom when suitable and at the top when it would compete with important bottom content. Use inline search for an obvious local collection. Clearly distinguish changed scope from changed query. Scopes are named categories; removable tokens represent particular terms or filters. Keep user history private and editable. See [journey patterns](patterns-journeys.md) for result states and context restoration.

## Commands

- **Button:** style, content, and role are separate. Label the action or choose a familiar symbol; one primary action should look primary by treatment, not by being a much larger hit target than its peers. Support default, pressed, focus, disabled, and pending states. Preserve size and identity when an activity indicator comes in. Don't use the primary/default role for a destructive action; make Cancel safe and easy to find. Apple's iOS button sizing table includes mini 28, small 32, regular 44, large 52 and extra large 64 pt; those are system visual sizes, not a reason to shrink routine phone hit areas below 44 × 44.
- **Pull-down and More menus:** related commands at a named trigger. Put frequent choices first and group by meaning. Keep hierarchy shallow and label choices with the action; a checkmark can communicate a current mode. Ordinary menus may show a disabled action to preserve discoverability. A label ellipsis in a menu can signal that more information is required to finish the action, not just that another view opens.
- **Context menu:** secondary, object-specific actions via touch-and-hold or secondary click. Keep only relevant actions here (hide unavailable ones), keep destructive actions clearly marked, and expose essential commands elsewhere. A preview should identify the target from opening through dismissal. Don't attach both an edit menu and a context menu to the same iPhone item.
- **Edit menu:** use the platform's select/copy/paste/undo behavior and place rare custom editing commands near related ones. Don't override browser or OS text editing for custom input. A home-screen quick action is different again: highly useful, stable app entry with a clear title and familiar icon; not a substitute for an in-app button.

Native bars and menus handle many defaults (back gesture, mirroring, text editing). Web destinations should be links where they represent URLs, with real browser history; commands are buttons. Provide visible focus and keyboard-accessible reveal, selection, and dismissal. A route navigation bar doesn't become an ARIA tablist just because it looks like one.

## For lmthing

Use code-owned `TabBar` for destinations, `Header` for orienting chrome, `Search` for a query, `Segmented` for local peer views, `Button` for one clear action, and `Actions` for action groups. Don't promote action-sheet and context-menu behavior to model-writable destinations. Audit host history/tab persistence, compact toolbar overflow and query preservation; these are kit or screen-runtime capabilities, not more variants of `Header`. Test address and focus restoration on a detail return across places, commerce, and workspace flows.
